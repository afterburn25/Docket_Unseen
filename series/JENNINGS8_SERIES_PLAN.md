# Jennings 8 / Jeff Davis 8 — Multi-Part Series Plan

## Series Goal

Treat the Jennings 8 / Jeff Davis 8 as a **long-form Docket Unseen documentary series**, not a single compressed episode.

The series should aim to be as comprehensive as practical: every verified victim detail, timeline event, witness connection, suspect/person-of-interest development, investigative controversy, disputed theory, related death, later development, and present-day status should be documented in the research dossier before scripting.

Because ElevenLabs and production workflows work best with smaller narration sections, **no individual narration block should approach the platform limit**. Target roughly **7,500–9,000 characters per generated narration section**, leaving room for revisions and pronunciation fixes.

A single YouTube episode may contain more than one ElevenLabs narration block if needed, but the Jennings 8 project itself should be released as a multi-part series rather than one oversized video.

## Research First — No Script Until the Dossier Exists

Before writing Part 1, build a master fact ledger with:

- exact victim names and aliases;
- ages;
- family / children / personal background;
- last-known sightings;
- missing-person reports;
- recovery dates and locations;
- condition / cause / manner of death when officially established;
- autopsy / coroner information that is public and appropriate to discuss;
- who discovered each victim;
- relationships among victims;
- relationships to witnesses, suspects, informants, dealers, sex-work networks, law enforcement, and other key people;
- arrests and charges connected to individual deaths;
- dropped charges and reasons when documented;
- persons of interest;
- search warrants;
- physical evidence;
- vehicles / locations;
- witness statements;
- informant claims;
- investigative task-force actions;
- evidence-handling controversies;
- recantations / conflicting statements;
- deaths or disappearances of witnesses or connected people;
- alleged law-enforcement conflicts or misconduct;
- official responses / denials;
- Ethan Brown reporting;
- other major reporting;
- Murder in the Bayou material;
- subsequent deaths of major figures;
- later law-enforcement statements;
- current unsolved status;
- every major theory, with source attribution and counterpoints.

Every item should be tagged as one of:

- **ESTABLISHED FACT**
- **OFFICIAL FINDING**
- **REPORTED CLAIM**
- **DISPUTED CLAIM**
- **UNRESOLVED THEORY**
- **UNKNOWN / NOT VERIFIED**

Do not present corruption, conspiracy, police involvement, or guilt as established unless supported by reliable evidence.

## Proposed Series Structure

This structure is intentionally expansive and may grow during research.

### Part 1 — Jennings Before the Eight

Purpose:
Establish Jennings / Jefferson Davis Parish, the social environment, geography, poverty, drugs, sex work, law-enforcement structure, and the network that connected many of the women.

Cover:
- Jennings and Jefferson Davis Parish context;
- neighborhoods and major locations;
- relevant local drug / sex-work environment;
- relationships among victims before the murders;
- informant culture, where reliably sourced;
- Sheila Comeaux and other potentially relevant pre-2005 events as **possible context**, not automatically part of the eight;
- why the later deaths were initially treated as separate cases.

End on the discovery of the first victim.

### Part 2 — Loretta Chaisson and Ernestine Daniels Patterson

Cover each woman as a person before discussing the crime.

Include:
- biography;
- family;
- last known movements;
- disappearance;
- discovery;
- recovery scene;
- forensic findings that are public;
- witnesses;
- known associates;
- investigation;
- arrests / charges connected to Patterson;
- dismissal of charges and documented reasons;
- links to later victims.

### Part 3 — Kristen Gary Lopez and Whitnei Dubois

Include:
- full personal background;
- final known days;
- missing timeline;
- discovery;
- forensic findings;
- social connections;
- witness accounts;
- vehicles / locations;
- investigative actions;
- people questioned;
- evidence disputes;
- links to earlier and later victims.

### Part 4 — Laconia “Muggy” Brown and Crystal Shay Benoit Zeno

Include:
- personal histories;
- family accounts;
- timeline;
- discoveries;
- forensic information;
- victim connections;
- reported drug / informant relationships where supported;
- key witnesses;
- suspects / persons of interest;
- investigative decisions.

### Part 5 — Brittney Gary and Necole Guillory

Brittney Gary was the youngest of the eight, so handle her coverage with additional care.

Include:
- personal histories;
- final sightings;
- disappearance timelines;
- discoveries;
- evidence;
- connections;
- Necole Guillory's reported fear that she might become a victim, with exact source attribution;
- what each woman may have known, only where reliably sourced;
- what investigators said publicly.

Finish with the realization that eight connected women had now died.

### Part 6 — The Web Connecting the Eight

This part should visually map the network.

Cover:
- which victims knew one another;
- shared houses / streets / social circles;
- drug-market connections;
- sex-work connections;
- alleged informant relationships;
- shared witnesses;
- shared suspects / persons of interest;
- common recovery-site characteristics;
- similarities and differences between deaths;
- what evidence supports a serial-offender theory;
- what evidence complicates a single-killer theory.

Use a large animated relationship map.

### Part 7 — Frankie Richard and Other Major Persons of Interest

Careful language is essential.

Cover:
- documented background;
- relationship to victims;
- law-enforcement statements;
- why he became a person of interest;
- searches / allegations / arrests unrelated or related to the murders;
- his denials;
- what was and was not proven;
- other recurring names and persons of interest;
- distinguish rumor from record.

Do not imply guilt without evidence.

### Part 8 — Fear, Witnesses, Informants, and People Who Died

A major through-line should be that **several women appeared to believe they were in danger before they died**.

Document separately:
- Muggy Brown's family statement that she believed "her time had come";
- Brittney Gary's expressed concern for her safety and distrust of people around her;
- Necole Guillory's repeated prediction that she would not live long;
- Loretta fear/informant claims if primary records support them.

Then ask — without assuming the answer — whether this fear came from:
- seeing women in the same social circle die;
- specific threats;
- involvement as informants;
- being questioned about earlier killings;
- firsthand knowledge;
- or general danger associated with the drug/sex-work environment.

Use `research/jennings8/FEAR_AND_FOREKNOWLEDGE.md`.

### Part 8 — Witnesses, Informants, and People Who Died

Cover:
- key witnesses;
- people who changed statements;
- reported informants;
- witnesses or connected individuals who later died;
- whether those deaths were officially linked or merely theorized;
- intimidation claims;
- reliability issues;
- how witness instability affected the investigation.

This episode should repeatedly distinguish **confirmed connection** from **theory**.

### Part 8B — Were There More Than Eight?

Optional standalone episode if research supports it.

Cover associated cases that became folded into the Jennings 8 narrative:
- Sheila Comeaux;
- Leonard Crochet;
- Steven Gunter;
- Harvey "Bird Dog" Burleigh;
- Russell Carrier;
- Lacie Fontenot;
- other parish unsolved homicides only where materially relevant.

For each, distinguish:
- documented case facts;
- official connection/no-connection statements;
- family/journalist theory;
- whether the person had information about the eight.

Do not inflate the Jennings 8 victim count. Use `research/jennings8/ASSOCIATED_DEATHS_AND_INCIDENTS.md`.

### Part 9 — Evidence, Crime Scenes, and Investigative Problems

Cover:
- crime-scene handling;
- evidence collection;
- chain-of-custody issues where documented;
- lost / mishandled / contaminated evidence allegations;
- vehicles and physical evidence;
- forensic limitations caused by decomposition;
- investigative delays;
- conflicting agency roles;
- task-force formation;
- mistakes alleged by journalists/families;
- law-enforcement explanations or responses.

### Part 10 — Law Enforcement, Alleged Corruption, Political/Institutional Questions, and Competing Accounts

This should be one of the most carefully sourced episodes.

Cover:
- sheriff's office structure during the murders;
- named officials relevant to the investigation;
- documented conflicts / disciplinary histories where relevant;
- allegations raised by families, witnesses, journalists, or documentaries;
- official denials / alternative explanations;
- any proven misconduct separated from unproven murder-related theories;
- claims involving informants;
- why some observers believe institutional problems impeded the cases;
- reasons others reject broader conspiracy theories.

Never narrate an unresolved corruption theory as fact.

This part should explicitly distinguish:
- proven ethics violations / disciplinary actions;
- documented investigative failures;
- missing or mishandled evidence;
- conflicts of interest;
- informant / sexual-relationship allegations;
- claims of deliberate cover-up;
- elected-office / political oversight questions;
- official denials and alternative explanations.

Use `research/jennings8/COVERUP_AND_FAILURES.md` as the fact ledger.

### Part 10B — Witnesses, Informants, and the "People Who Knew Too Much" Pattern

If research is deep enough, split this from Part 10.

Investigate person-by-person:
- victims questioned about earlier deaths;
- informants;
- witnesses who recanted;
- people who later died;
- Frankie Richard's later statements and 2020 death;
- whether any later death was officially linked to the case.

Do not claim that "everyone who came forward died" unless the evidence supports it. Use `research/jennings8/WITNESSES_INFORMANTS_AND_DEATHS.md`.

### Part 11 — Ethan Brown, Murder in the Bayou, and the Case Re-Examined

Cover:
- Ethan Brown's reporting;
- his book;
- sources he relied on;
- claims he advanced;
- reaction from Jennings / law enforcement;
- Showtime's Murder in the Bayou;
- what the series brought back into public attention;
- criticisms / limitations;
- developments that occurred after publication / broadcast.

### Part 12 — Theories: One Killer, Multiple Killers, or Something Else?

Lay out competing theories separately.

Potential theory buckets:
- one serial killer;
- multiple unrelated killers;
- killings connected to the drug trade;
- killings tied to informant activity;
- witness elimination;
- involvement by people within victims' social network;
- institutional cover-up theories;
- combinations of the above.

For each:
- evidence cited by proponents;
- evidence against;
- what is speculation;
- what remains unexplained.

Do **not** pick a theory as the answer unless the evidence warrants it.

### Part 13 — Where the Case Stands Now

Cover:
- later deaths of important figures;
- any later investigative activity;
- law-enforcement status;
- rewards / tip lines if current;
- whether DNA or newer forensic techniques have been discussed;
- what evidence may still exist;
- what families have said in later years;
- what questions remain unanswered.

End the series with the eight women, not the suspects.

## Victim-Centered Rule

Every victim gets meaningful screen and narration time as a person, not merely a numbered body in the case.

For each victim, the research dossier should include:
- full name;
- preferred / commonly used name;
- age;
- children / family where publicly appropriate;
- interests / personality from family accounts;
- photograph sourcing;
- timeline;
- known connections;
- investigative facts;
- what remains unknown.

Avoid reducing the women to drug use or sex work. Those facts may be relevant to the investigation but should not define their entire portrayal.

## Narration / Character Limits

For ElevenLabs:
- target 7,500–9,000 characters per generation block;
- never wait until the hard limit;
- split at natural scene / chapter boundaries;
- save every narration block in GitHub;
- number blocks sequentially, e.g. `P03_Narration_A.md`, `P03_Narration_B.md`;
- combine audio only after each block is approved.

Each source entry should say exactly what claim it supports.

## Archival / Real Video Plan

The Jennings 8 series should use real footage extensively where legally usable.

Look for:
- sheriff / police interviews;
- recorded witness interviews;
- local-news interviews with family members;
- press conferences;
- archival broadcasts from 2005–2009;
- task-force announcements;
- footage of recovery locations and Jennings neighborhoods;
- interviews with journalists, investigators, attorneys, and people connected to the case;
- courtroom or hearing footage if public;
- public-record video and law-enforcement releases;
- clips from later reporting used only when rights / fair-use considerations are addressed.

Each clip should be logged in the research dossier with:
- source / owner;
- date;
- people shown;
- what factual point it supports;
- approximate clip duration used;
- rights / licensing / public-record status;
- whether it needs an on-screen source credit.

Do not use long uninterrupted clips as filler. Real footage should be cut against narration, timelines, maps, captions, document excerpts, and original analysis.

For disputed claims, the viewer should ideally see the **actual person making the claim** when footage exists, rather than hearing Docket Unseen state the allegation as fact.

## Production Goal

This should be treated as a flagship Docket Unseen series.

Do not rush it.

If research shows that one proposed part needs two episodes, split it again rather than deleting important verified material.

The goal is not literal repetition of every rumor ever published. The goal is to preserve **every materially relevant, verifiable detail and every major disputed theory**, while clearly labeling uncertainty and avoiding unsupported accusations.
