# Jennings 8 — Cover-Up Allegations & Investigative Failures Ledger

## Purpose

This file tracks the strongest evidence for:
- documented investigative failures;
- proven misconduct;
- disputed cover-up allegations;
- alleged law-enforcement conflicts;
- political / institutional questions;
- official responses and denials.

**Series rule:** Docket Unseen may explore whether institutional failure or a cover-up impeded the investigation, but must never present an unproven conspiracy as established fact.

## 1. Warren Gary and the white truck

### What is documented
A surviving copy of Louisiana Board of Ethics Opinion **2007-489**, dated June 26, 2008, states:
- Warren Gary had been employed by the Jefferson Davis Parish Sheriff's Office since 1992 and had served as Chief Criminal Investigator since 1997;
- on March 20, 2007, a suspect was brought to the Sheriff's Office for questioning in an ongoing criminal investigation;
- during questioning, Gary negotiated to buy her vehicle;
- the suspect was arrested that afternoon;
- Gary bought the vehicle on March 30 for **$8,748.90**;
- the suspect used **$3,207.13** of proceeds to pay the Jefferson Davis Parish District Attorney's Office for bad checks;
- Gary sold the vehicle on April 20 for **$15,500**;
- the Board found he violated Sections 1115B and 1116A of Louisiana's Code of Governmental Ethics;
- Gary consented to the findings and a **$10,000 penalty**.

KPLC later reported Gary was pulled from the Jeff Davis 8 investigation after the transaction came to light.

Separate witness / journalistic claims connected the same vehicle to the Kristen Lopez investigation. **The Ethics Board ruling itself does not establish that the truck carried Lopez's body or that Gary knew it contained murder evidence.**

Source copy:
Louisiana Board of Ethics, Opinion 2007-489 (surviving copy uploaded online; direct state archive copy still preferred).

### Why it matters
A lead investigator personally buying and later selling a vehicle that may have had evidentiary value creates an obvious conflict and chain-of-custody problem even if no criminal intent is proven.

### Official / counter view
Jefferson Davis Parish Chief Deputy Chris Ivey later said he did not believe Gary knew the vehicle contained evidence when he bought it.

### Status
**DOCUMENTED MISCONDUCT / ETHICS ISSUE.**
Any claim that Gary intentionally destroyed murder evidence remains **DISPUTED / UNPROVEN**.

Sources:
- KPLC: "Police, investigator swabbed for DNA in Jeff Davis case"
- Oxygen / Murder in the Bayou reporting
- Louisiana Board of Ethics record still needs direct retrieval.

## 2. Missing evidence / Paula Guillory

### Reported facts
KPLC reported on August 13, 2009:
- Deputy Paula Guillory had been suspended after evidence went missing;
- Sheriff Ricky Edwards asked the Vermilion Parish Sheriff's Office to investigate;
- the investigation found more than **$3,700** missing;
- Edwards fired Guillory and notified prosecutors.

A 2020 civil-rights complaint by the Promise of Justice Initiative later described broader missing evidence, including jewelry, drugs, and cash, and connected the evidence problem to collapse of a criminal case involving Frankie Richard.

Important:
- KPLC's contemporaneous article directly verifies the missing-money/firing event;
- broader claims about what other evidence disappeared and which case it affected need court/agency records.

KPLC contemporaneous reporting also documented community concerns over law-enforcement evidence handling in the Jeff Davis investigation.

### Why it matters
This does not prove evidence in the eight murders was intentionally destroyed, but it is relevant to evaluating the competence/integrity of evidence systems surrounding the same agencies and people.

### Status
**DOCUMENTED ALLEGATION / PERSONNEL ACTION; PRIMARY PERSONNEL/COURT RECORDS STILL NEEDED.**

## 3. Jesse Ewing and the inmate recordings

### Documented timeline
KPLC published a Louisiana State Police statement on December 19, 2007 saying:
- Jennings Police Sgt. Jesse Ewing released confidential information to civilian private investigator Kirk Menard;
- the material related to an ongoing investigation by another agency;
- State Police arrested Ewing on obstruction/malfeasance-related allegations.

KPLC's next-day reporting said:
- two female inmates had requested to speak to Ewing about the four then-known deaths;
- Menard said each inmate separately alleged a cover-up involving a **"high-elected parish official"**;
- Menard delivered the recordings to the FBI and Louisiana Attorney General's Office;
- Menard explicitly cautioned that he was not claiming the cover-up allegation was true.

KPLC later reported Ewing pleaded guilty in 2008 to misdemeanor criminal mischief after the case was reduced.

Ewing publicly maintained that he believed he had done the right thing.

### Why it matters
This is a real, documented incident in which alleged cover-up information existed inside law enforcement and the officer who removed it from normal channels was prosecuted for doing so.

### What it does NOT prove
It does not establish that the inmates' allegations were true.

### Status
**ESTABLISHED EVENT; COVER-UP CONTENT REMAINS ALLEGATION.**

Sources:
- KPLC "Officer Arrested: His Side of the Story"
- KPLC "Former Jennings police officer running for Jeff Davis sheriff"

## 4. DNA swabbing of investigators

### Documented
KPLC reported in late 2009 that police/investigators associated with the Jeff Davis cases were being swabbed for DNA under a policy announced by Sheriff Ricky Edwards.

### Why it matters
The testing reflected enough concern / investigative need that law-enforcement personnel were included in DNA elimination/comparison procedures.

### What is unknown
- full list of people swabbed;
- results;
- whether results were purely elimination samples or related to specific suspicions.

### Status
**OFFICIAL INVESTIGATIVE ACTION; RESULTS NOT PUBLICLY ESTABLISHED.**

## 5. Law-enforcement relationships with victims

### Investigative reporting
Ethan Brown and the Showtime series reported that:
- several victims served as law-enforcement informants;
- some had sexual or otherwise intimate relationships with officers / jail personnel;
- first victim Loretta Chaisson-Lewis was alleged by a former cellmate to have had sex with jail warden Terrie Guillory.

### Official response
Sheriff Ricky Edwards declined to confirm informant identities, saying he would protect their anonymity if they were informants.

### Status
**PARTLY CORROBORATED JOURNALISTIC CLAIM; VICTIM-BY-VICTIM PRIMARY VERIFICATION REQUIRED.**

## 7. Political / institutional dimension

The word "political corruption" appears in major media descriptions of `Murder in the Bayou`, but Docket Unseen should not leap from that phrase to an assertion that named elected officials participated in murder or a cover-up.

### Elected / political institutions relevant to research
- Jefferson Davis Parish Sheriff (elected office)
- Jefferson Davis Parish District Attorney / 31st Judicial District
- Louisiana Attorney General
- parish / municipal leadership where relevant
- Louisiana State Police and state oversight mechanisms

### Attorney General Buddy Caldwell
In 2014, after renewed calls for state intervention, Louisiana Attorney General Buddy Caldwell told KPLC his office had **no jurisdiction or control** to take over the Jeff Davis 8 investigation in the manner some citizens requested.

### Research questions
- Were any elected officials accused by named witnesses or records of interfering?
- Were there campaign, family, business, or patronage ties relevant to people of interest?
- What did the DA's office know about failed prosecutions?
- What oversight authority actually existed?
- Were referrals made to state/federal authorities?
- What happened to those referrals?

### Status
**POLITICAL/INSTITUTIONAL QUESTIONS — NOT PROOF OF POLITICAL COVER-UP.**

## 8. Official denials of cover-up

Law-enforcement officials have repeatedly denied a cover-up.

Examples:
- Sheriff Ricky Edwards publicly denied that he or other sheriffs would cover up wrongdoing.
- Later Jefferson Davis Parish Sheriff's Office statements called Ethan Brown's theory a "fictional conspiracy theory" and said there had been "no cover up."

### Series requirement
Every episode that presents a cover-up allegation must also present the relevant official denial or alternative explanation.

## Working documentary question

A fair framing for Docket Unseen is:

> **Were the Jennings 8 cases doomed by ordinary investigative failure, compromised by conflicts and misconduct, deliberately obstructed, or some combination of those factors?**

That is a question the series can investigate.

It is **not** currently an established conclusion.
