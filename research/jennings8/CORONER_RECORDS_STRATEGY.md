# Jennings 8 — Coroner & Death-Record Acquisition Strategy

## Purpose

The victim dossiers currently rely heavily on KPLC retrospective summaries for cause/manner of death.

Louisiana law gives us a better route:
request the **public coroner's report** for each woman.

This can establish authoritative:
- date/time of death;
- place of death;
- date/time of autopsy, where applicable;
- cause of death;
- manner of death;
- scientifically contributing factors.

This is not the same thing as obtaining the full autopsy report.

This is research strategy, not legal advice.

---

## Current Louisiana law — R.S. 44:19

Official:
https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=99658

### Coroner's report — public

R.S. 44:19(E)(3) defines a **coroner's report** and says it is a public record.

It includes:
- decedent name;
- address;
- sex;
- DOB;
- age;
- race;
- date/time of death;
- place of death;
- date/time of autopsy, when applicable;
- **cause and manner of death**;
- scientifically contributing factors.

This is our primary public-record target.

### Full death-investigation report — not public

R.S. 44:19(E)(2):
- death investigation report is internal coroner work product;
- not a public document;
- available to specified relatives / law enforcement / subpoena routes.

### Full autopsy report — generally not public

R.S. 44:19(E)(4):
- full post-mortem forensic medical examination/autopsy report is generally not public;
- available to specified relatives and certain other authorized persons;
- subject to subpoena/court order;
- special rules apply if used in criminal/death-in-custody investigation.

### Autopsy photos/video
Confidential except statutory exceptions.

Docket Unseen should not seek graphic autopsy imagery for the series.

---

# What to request for each woman

Request:

> the public coroner's report under R.S. 44:19(E)(3)

Ask for:
- cause;
- manner;
- contributing factors;
- date/time of death;
- place of death;
- date/time of autopsy;
- coroner/pathologist name if shown.

If the office says no coroner report exists:
- ask for the record custodian and whether a public fact-of-death letter or other public coroner document exists.

Do not begin by requesting graphic photos or the internal death-investigation report.

# Draft request

Subject:
Public coroner's report request — [victim name]

Body:

Hello,

Pursuant to Louisiana R.S. 44:19(E)(3), I am requesting a copy of the public **coroner's report** for:

[full name]
Date of death/body recovery: [date]
Approximate location: [location]

I am specifically requesting the public coroner's report containing the cause and manner of death and related information described in R.S. 44:19(E)(3).

I am not requesting autopsy photographs or confidential medical-history information.

Electronic production is preferred.

If your office is not the custodian of this record, please let me know, if possible, which coroner/autopsy office has custody.

Thank you.

# Why this is analytically powerful

Coroner reports could immediately correct several current uncertainties:

- whether all eight were legally classified as homicides;
- which deaths share asphyxiation;
- which were undetermined;
- whether drug intoxication was cause, contributor, or incidental;
- whether a real cross-victim mechanism exists.

That information can strengthen or weaken:
- one-offender theory;
- multiple-offender theory;
- copycat/mixed theory;
- official "eight murders" shorthand.

# Rules for final video

1. Use the coroner's official cause/manner language.
2. Do not embellish injuries for drama.
3. Avoid graphic images.
4. If cause is "undetermined," say **undetermined**.
5. Distinguish cause of death from manner of death.
6. If toxicology contributed, explain it accurately rather than implying overdose unless the report says so.
