# Jennings 8 — Part 6 Research: The Web Connecting the Eight

## Episode purpose

After Parts 1–5 establish the town and all eight women, Part 6 should answer the viewer's obvious question:

> **Were these eight deaths connected because one killer targeted them — or because the women, suspects, witnesses, drugs, informants, and law enforcement were all part of the same tightly connected network?**

This episode should be built around a **large animated relationship map** whose lines appear only when the narration establishes the supporting evidence.

## Core official anchor

### December 2008
Sheriff Ricky Edwards:
- formed a full-time multi-agency team after seven deaths;
- said cases would be investigated "individually and collectively";
- used "killer or killers";
- said evidence did not yet justify certainty that all cases were linked.

### October 2009
Task force:
- said all eight were being treated as murders unless proven otherwise;
- said the collective agency view was that they **may have been committed by a common offender**;
- said no single suspect was being exclusively targeted;
- reported nearly 1,000 leads;
- approximately 500 interviews;
- more than 48,000 investigative hours;
- raised reward from $35,000 to $85,000.

These official statements create the narrative tension:
**one common offender was a serious theory, but investigators publicly kept multiple suspects open.**

## Geographic hub

KPLC 2009 South Main reporting gives a strong contemporaneous anchor:
- Necole last seen on Doyle Street near South Main;
- local residents said they knew many/all of the women;
- residents described seeing women walking the same streets and getting into cars;
- task force said offender may have discontinued contact with the area formerly frequented by victims.

This should be animated as the first layer of the map:
**South Main / Doyle Street → victims.**

## Social hub

Later A&E/Washington Post/Brown reporting:
- women knew one another;
- Kristen and Brittney were related;
- many shared drug/sex-work networks;
- Boudreaux Inn appears repeatedly;
- Frankie Richard knew many/all of them.

This is the second layer:
**victims ↔ victims ↔ Richard ↔ Boudreaux/social network.**

## Informant layer

Brown/A&E says:
- all eight had worked as police informants.

This is potentially one of the most important common links in the entire case.

But because Docket Unseen lacks official CI records for all eight:
- show this layer in amber/dashed lines;
- label **REPORTED INFORMANT CONNECTIONS**;
- name only victim-specific informant claims that have family/reporting support;
- state that sheriff/law enforcement did not publicly confirm individual CI identities.

Do not answer yet.

## Recovery-site overlay

Show all eight body locations.

Questions:
- water vs roadside/field;
- same general geographic familiarity;
- whether sites indicate offender with local knowledge;
- Necole's I-10 placement as change in pattern.

Use task-force statements as official source for behavioral interpretation.

## Competing interpretations

### Interpretation A — one common offender
Official 2009 task-force working theory.

### Interpretation B — multiple offenders
Brown's later interpretation based on individual suspect/prosecution histories.

### Interpretation C — mixed scenario
One offender responsible for some, others copied/used similar disposal patterns, or several killers operated within same social network.

No interpretation should be declared solved.

## End of Part 6

End with the relationship map nearly full.

Then remove every speculative/dashed connection.

Leave only solid documented links.

Narration concept:
> "Even after the rumors are stripped away, the network that remains is still unusually dense."

Then transition to:
**Frankie Richard and the major persons of interest.**
