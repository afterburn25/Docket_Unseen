# Jennings 8 — Evidence-Based Case Cluster Analysis

## Purpose

This file tests whether the eight cases naturally separate into subsets using documented investigative relationships, not intuition or guilt assumptions.

It combines official timelines, prosecution history, victim relationships, geography, vehicles, fear/informant evidence, the 2013 Orion study, and known custody exclusions.

This is not a finding that any subset had the same killer.

## Method

A cluster is meaningful only when at least two independent evidence classes overlap.

Evidence classes: official/court; contemporaneous reporting; family/witness; later investigative journalism; computational/Orion.

No numerical guilt or suspect scores are used.

## Cluster A — Kristen Lopez / Brittney Gary

- Kristen and Brittney were cousins.
- KPLC said Brittney was friends with several earlier victims.
- The 2013 Orion study says the terms Kristen and Brittnei mapped near one another and concludes those investigations tended to be closely related.
- This may reflect shared witnesses, contacts, locations, or suspects; it does not prove the same offender.

Status: STRONGEST PUBLICLY SUPPORTED VICTIM-PAIR CLUSTER.

## Cluster B — Laconia "Muggy" Brown as a distinct investigative stream

- The Orion study says Laconia mapped far from Kristen/Brittney, indicating some difference in the conducted investigation.
- An anonymized surname stem, ln1, appeared frequently because many people with that surname were interrogated in Necole's investigation and was close to the Laconia region.
- The masked surname must not be de-anonymized from speculation.

Status: COMPUTATIONALLY DISTINCT INVESTIGATIVE CLUSTER / CAUSE UNKNOWN.

## Cluster C — Ernestine Patterson prosecution cluster

- Ernestine's case produced the Jones/Nixon prosecution.
- It had its own witness, possible scene, vehicle-fiber, and prosecution history.
- A 2018 appellate opinion shows prosecutors were still seeking homicide information from Nixon in 2016, though the opinion does not name the victim.

Status: STRONG PROCEDURAL / PROSECUTION CLUSTER.

## Cluster D — Kristen / Whitnei / Frankie Richard social-investigative cluster

- KPLC said Richard was interviewed in three of the first four deaths and had been seen with at least three of the first four women near their final days.
- KNOE later reported Richard had relationships with six of the eight and was last to see at least two alive.
- Ethan Brown especially emphasizes Richard's proximity to Kristen and Whitnei.
- There is still no public forensic proof tying Richard to a homicide.

Status: STRONG SOCIAL / INVESTIGATIVE CLUSTER, NOT FORENSICALLY PROVEN.

## Cluster E — Fear / foreknowledge / possible witness-information group

- Loretta: later family says she was preparing to provide drug-related information and appeared frightened.
- Muggy: contemporaneous family statement says she feared she was next.
- Brittney: contemporaneous family statement says she feared for her safety and did not know whom to trust.
- Necole: mother said she repeatedly predicted she would not live long; AP confirms she had been a witness in a 2002 parish-jail misconduct investigation.

Status: REAL FEAR PATTERN; COMMON MOTIVE UNPROVEN.

## Cluster F — Final-four / task-force common-offender phase

Victims: Muggy, Crystal, Brittney, Necole.

- These deaths came after earlier Patterson and Lopez prosecutions had failed.
- Public fear was higher and the task force increasingly used common-offender language.
- Body-placement pattern had shifted away from the first-three canal pattern.
- Jeffery Lee Guillory's incarceration from Jan. 25, 2008 hard-excludes him from this subset.

Status: INVESTIGATIVE-PHASE CLUSTER, NOT PROVEN OFFENDER CLUSTER.

## Cluster G — First-three canal recoveries

Victims: Loretta, Ernestine, Kristen.

- All three were recovered in canals/water.
- Whitnei marks the first clear roadside break.
- Ernestine and Kristen nevertheless produced different suspect/prosecution clusters.

Status: GEOGRAPHIC / DISPOSAL CLUSTER ONLY.

## Cluster H — Law-enforcement / institutional-information overlap

- Necole: documented 2002 jail-misconduct witness.
- Loretta: family says she expected to provide information in a drug matter.
- Muggy: later reporting says she provided information on earlier murders.
- Crystal: family later believed she was an informant.

This is an information/access layer, not a homicide-offender cluster.

## What the Orion study adds

The peer-reviewed study explicitly says:
- 172 Necole-related Information Packages were analyzed.
- Three distinct text clusters emerged among subsets of the records.
- Kristen/Brittney investigations tended to be closely related.
- Laconia's investigative material differed.
- Anonymized recurring family/name stems emerged.
- Geographic clustering was only partially aligned with text clustering.
- The task force said the analysis revealed previously unknown information / potentially important clues.

This shows investigative sub-networks, not offender identities.

## Working analytical model

At the current public-evidence level, it is more defensible to think in overlapping subsets than to force one model across all eight.

Most meaningful current subsets:
1. Kristen ↔ Brittney — family + Orion relationship.
2. Patterson prosecution cluster — Jones/Nixon.
3. Kristen / Whitnei / Richard — social/investigative overlap.
4. Muggy distinct Orion stream.
5. Fear / knowledge cluster — Loretta, Muggy, Brittney, Necole.
6. Final-four investigative phase.
7. First-three canal cluster.

## What would turn clusters into offender groups

Needed: shared unknown DNA; same vehicle/phone/contact; same verified last companion; shared distinctive forensic signature; one suspect's verified access to multiple victims; or corroborated confession with nonpublic details.

Without those, these remain investigative clusters, not killer clusters.
