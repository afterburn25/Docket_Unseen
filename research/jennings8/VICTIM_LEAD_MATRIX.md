# Jennings 8 — Victim-by-Victim Lead / Opportunity Matrix

## Purpose

This matrix is an **investigative comparison tool**, not a guilt chart.

It tracks:
- confirmed last-seen / recovery facts;
- named public investigative leads;
- the nature of each link;
- what supports the lead;
- what weakens or contradicts it;
- the missing record that would most change the analysis.

### Evidence labels

- **OFFICIAL / PRIMARY** — court, agency, coroner, ethics, contemporaneous official statement.
- **CONTEMPORANEOUS REPORTING** — local reporting from the active investigation quoting named officials/witnesses.
- **FAMILY / WITNESS CLAIM** — attributed firsthand/family statement.
- **LATER INVESTIGATIVE REPORTING** — Ethan Brown / documentary / later reporting based on interviews or claimed case files.
- **UNVERIFIED THEORY** — claim not yet tied to an underlying primary statement or record.

**No entry in this file means Docket Unseen believes that person committed a murder.**

# Victim 1 — Loretta Lynn Chaisson-Lewis

## Fixed timeline
- Last seen: **May 17, 2005**
- Found: **May 20, 2005**
- Recovery: Grand Marais / canal area off LA 1126 in later KPLC reporting
- Official age: 28

## Lead A — Frankie Richard

### Supporting public information
**FAMILY / WITNESS CLAIM**
- Later family/documentary reporting says Loretta's brother recalled seeing her enter a vehicle with Frankie Richard shortly before she disappeared.

**CONTEMPORANEOUS CROSS-CASE CONTEXT**
- By 2007 Sheriff Ricky Edwards said Richard had been interviewed in three of the first four deaths and had been seen with at least three of the women during their final days. KPLC did not identify every one of those three in that article.

### Weaknesses
- Docket Unseen does not yet have the original Loretta last-seen witness statement.
- No public charge tied Richard to Loretta's death.
- No public forensic evidence ties Richard to her death.
- Richard denied involvement in the eight killings.

### Critical missing evidence
1. original last-seen report / witness statement;
2. Loretta phone records;
3. Richard interview regarding Loretta;
4. vehicle description;
5. autopsy / toxicology / manner;
6. DNA / trace evidence.

## Lead B — informant / drug-witness motive

### Supporting public information
**FAMILY / LATER INVESTIGATIVE REPORTING**
- Loretta's family later said she had discussed acting as a witness/informant in a drug matter.
- Family/friend accounts describe Loretta acting frightened shortly before disappearing.

### Weaknesses
- exact agency/case remains unrecovered;
- no CI documentation in public dossier;
- no named threat linked to that information.

### Investigative value
This may matter more as a **motive line** than as an offender identity.

# Victim 3 — Kristen E. Gary Lopez

## Fixed timeline
- Last seen: **March 6, 2007**
- Found: **March 18, 2007**
- Recovery: Petitjean Canal off LA 99
- Cause of death: contemporaneous KPLC reported **undetermined**
- Toxicology: elevated drugs/alcohol in KPLC report

## Lead A — Frankie Richard

### Supporting public information
**CONTEMPORANEOUS OFFICIAL REPORTING**
KPLC May 16, 2007 quoted Sheriff Ricky Edwards saying:
- an informant tip gave investigators enough evidence to arrest Richard and Hannah Conner;
- both faced **second-degree murder charges** in Kristen's death;
- Richard had been interviewed in multiple early cases;
- he had been seen with at least three victims during the final days of their lives.

### Major contradiction
**LATER OFFICIAL ACCOUNT REPORTED SECONDHAND**
- Years later the Sheriff's Office reportedly told another program Richard had not actually been arrested for Kristen's death and was jailed on a separate rape allegation.

This contradiction is unresolved until primary booking/warrant/DA records are obtained.

## Lead B — Hannah Conner

### Supporting public information
**CONTEMPORANEOUS OFFICIAL REPORTING**
- KPLC reports Conner arrested in Vermilion Parish in connection with Kristen's murder.
- Same article says she and Richard faced second-degree murder charges.

### Weaknesses
- no current primary charging/disposition file in dossier;
- later murder narratives depend heavily on contested witness/jailhouse statements.

## Lead C — Tracee Chaisson

### Supporting public information
**CONTEMPORANEOUS OFFICIAL REPORTING**
- Tracee reported Kristen missing.
- KPLC reported Tracee was booked as **accessory after the fact**.
- Investigators believed she knew where Kristen's body was when she made the report.

### Weaknesses
- accessory charge disposition not recovered;
- later Tracee interview says she had given false information and later recanted, according to documentary material;
- exact truth of statements remains unresolved.

## Evidence-conflict lead — white truck / Warren Gary

### Established
**PRIMARY ETHICS RECORD**
- Gary bought and rapidly resold a vehicle from a suspect he had questioned and admitted ethics violations.

### Disputed murder link
**LATER INVESTIGATIVE REPORTING**
- witnesses/inmates allegedly tied a white truck to Kristen's movements/body transport.

### Weaknesses
- Ethics ruling does not establish the truck was murder evidence;
- no public lab/scene record yet links the vehicle to Kristen.

## Critical missing evidence
1. Richard/Conner warrants and booking;
2. DA charging/disposition;
3. Tracee charge/disposition;
4. original informant tip;
5. white truck VIN/title/evidence testing;
6. autopsy / toxicology;
7. Ewing tapes;
8. Richard/Conner/Tracee interviews.

# Preliminary pattern observations — not conclusions

## Pattern 1 — First four contain the strongest named-suspect activity
- Ernestine had a formal prosecution.
- Kristen had explicit 2007 arrest/charge reporting.
- Richard was being investigated across the early cluster.
- Whitnei died amid that active arrest/investigation period.

This may mean:
- investigators had better witnesses early;
- the early cases truly involved separate local offenders;
- or public charging was premature and later collapsed.

## Pattern 2 — Final four shift toward fear / common-offender theory
- Muggy feared she was next.
- Crystal's death was formally treated as homicide in contemporaneous reporting.
- Brittney had a precise surveillance timeline and had expressed fear.
- Necole had predicted danger and was last seen entering an unknown vehicle.
- task force increasingly used common-offender language.

This may reflect:
- escalation by one offender;
- public fear changing witness behavior;
- or a shift in investigative framing after earlier prosecutions failed.

## Pattern 3 — The strongest public case for one killer is **not** a named suspect
The one-killer theory currently rests mostly on:
- geography;
- social overlap;
- disposal pattern;
- task-force behavioral inference.

It does not currently rest on a public DNA match or a single named person tied forensically to all eight.

## Pattern 4 — The strongest public case for multiple offenders is procedural
- Patterson generated one suspect group.
- Kristen generated another.
- later witness/informant leads point elsewhere.

But failed charges are not proof the charged people were guilty.

# Investigative rule

The matrix may be used to say:

> "This lead appears in more than one victim's case."

or:

> "This theory loses strength when compared with the timeline."

It must **not** be used to say:

> "This person is the killer"

without conclusive official/adjudicated evidence.
