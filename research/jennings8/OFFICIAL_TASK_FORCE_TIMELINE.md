# Jennings 8 — Official Task Force Position Timeline

## Purpose

This file records **what the multi-agency task force publicly said at the time**, in chronological order.

It is separate from:
- Ethan Brown's later interpretation;
- family theories;
- online discussion;
- Docket Unseen analysis.

This is the best way to show how the official theory changed.

---

# December 18, 2008 — Task force formation

KPLC preserved Sheriff Ricky Edwards' press-conference statement nearly verbatim.

Source:
https://www.kplctv.com/story/9550151/jennings-press-conference-on-mystery-murders/

At this point there were **seven** known victims.

## Official position

Edwards said:
- a full-time **Multi-Agency Investigative Team** was being formed;
- 8–10 investigators would work full-time on the seven deaths;
- cases would be worked **individually and collectively**;
- investigators did **not yet have enough facts to say with certainty that all cases were linked**;
- the mission was to catch the **"killer or killers"**;
- whoever was responsible for some or all cases was likely familiar with the Jennings area;
- investigators believed the offender or offenders had made mistakes;
- investigators wanted women who had experienced suspicious approaches/assaultive behavior to come forward.

## Initial offender characteristics publicly listed

Edwards described a possible offender as someone who might be:
- superficially charming;
- self-confident;
- initially non-threatening;
- physically strong;
- familiar with areas where women disappeared;
- quick to anger if rejected;
- willing to lure women with alcohol/drugs;
- possibly with assaultive or burglary history.

Important:
This was **behavioral guidance**, not an identification.

## Historical tip line
**337-824-6662**

Edwards also announced a website would be opened for tips.

## Agency count
Edwards said the team had investigators from **8 separate agencies** at formation, with additional statewide sheriff resources available.

# August 20, 2009 — after Necole Guillory became victim eight

KPLC preserved the task-force press release in full.

Source:
https://www.kplctv.com/story/10966767/body-identified-as-necole-guillory-of-jeff-davis-parish/

## Agencies listed publicly
The release specifically names:
- Acadia Parish Sheriff's Office
- Calcasieu Parish Sheriff's Office
- Jefferson Davis Parish Sheriff's Office
- Jennings Police Department
- 15th Judicial District Attorney's Office
- 31st Judicial District Attorney's Office
- Louisiana State Police
- Louisiana Attorney General's Office
- FBI

## Official classification
The task force called Necole:
- another female **homicide victim**.

## Shift to singular-offender language

The release repeatedly uses **"the offender."**

Task force said the offender:
- had changed method of operation;
- may have changed vehicle / cleaned vehicle / sold vehicle;
- likely followed media and police activity closely;
- likely had strong ties to Jennings / Jefferson Davis Parish;
- was familiar with the area;
- may have changed routine, transportation, work/social behavior;
- may have increased alcohol/drug use after killings.

## Necole disposal change
Task force specifically said:
- Necole's body being left beside Interstate 10 in Acadia Parish represented a change;
- previous bodies were left beside smaller rural roads.

This is the clearest official behavioral-analysis statement in the public record.

Important:
Singular language does **not** prove one offender existed.
It shows the task force was working from that behavioral model at the time.

# October 28, 2009 — major task-force update

KPLC preserved the task-force statement.

Source:
https://www.kplctv.com/story/11399926/jeff-davis-task-force-holds-news-conference/

## Named agency representatives present
- FBI New Orleans SAC David Welker
- Louisiana State Police Capt. Barry Branton
- Calcasieu Parish Sheriff Tony Mancuso
- Jennings Police Chief Johnny Lassiter
- Acadia Parish Sheriff Wayne Melancon
- Louisiana Department of Justice Director of Investigations Mike Thompson

Task force said additional agencies were also involved.

## Official classification of the eight

The statement said:
- task force was treating **all eight as murders unless it could prove otherwise**;
- collective opinion of participating agencies was that the murders **may have been committed by a common offender**;
- for that reason, "serial murder" was technically applicable as a working label;
- investigators intentionally avoided emphasizing the label because they believed it did not help identification/apprehension.

This is more committed to a common-offender framework than the December 2008 statement.

## Suspects / persons of interest

Task force said:
- several individuals had been considered possible suspects/persons of interest;
- **none had been ruled out completely**;
- no single suspect was the exclusive focus;
- investigators would continue considering other suspects.

This is crucial.

The public official position in Oct. 2009 was simultaneously:
1. **common offender may explain the cases**, and
2. **multiple suspects/persons of interest remained active**.

## Investigative scale

Task force reported:
- nearly **1,000 leads** followed;
- approximately **500 people interviewed**;
- countless phone calls;
- more than **48,000 investigative hours**;
- FBI lead-management system used to enter/recheck leads.

## Reward

Reward increased:
- from **$35,000**
- to **$85,000**

Funders publicly named:
- Jefferson Davis Parish District Attorney's Office
- Jefferson Davis Parish Sheriff's Office
- FBI

Lamar Advertising provided billboards.

## Missing-women work

Task force said:
- since Nov. 2008 it had also been tasked with locating approximately **30 reported missing females**.

This shows the team's activity extended beyond the eight case files.

## Public information portal

Historical website:
**www.jeffdaviscrimes.net**

Historical tip line:
**337-824-6662**

The site is now historical/offline for our purposes.

# October 26, 2009 — Edwards progress interview

KPLC:
https://www.kplctv.com/story/11387065/jeff-davis-sheriff-says-task-force-making-progress/

Edwards said:

# Evolution of official theory

## Stage 1 — 2005–07
Cases treated primarily individually.
Public reporting says investigators did not initially believe Loretta and Ernestine were connected.

## Stage 2 — Dec. 2008
Seven victims.
Task force created.
Official language:
- individual + collective;
- "killer or killers";
- not enough evidence to declare all linked.

## Stage 3 — Aug. 2009
Eight victims.
Behavioral profile uses singular "offender."
Local familiarity / vehicle changes / media monitoring emphasized.

## Stage 4 — Oct. 2009
Task force publicly says:
- all eight treated as murders unless proved otherwise;
- cases **may** involve common offender;
- no exclusive suspect;
- multiple persons of interest remain.

## Stage 5 — 2019
Sheriff Ivy Woods later told KPLC:
- not all eight had formally been ruled homicides;
- nothing official proved all eight were connected or caused by one serial killer.

This does not erase the 2009 task-force model.
It shows a later official retreat toward victim-specific classification.

---

# Analytical importance

The official record does **not** support a simplistic statement like:
> "Police always knew it was one serial killer."

A more accurate statement:

> "By late 2009 the multi-agency task force had adopted a strong common-offender working theory, while still keeping multiple suspects and alternative possibilities open."

Likewise, the record does **not** support:
> "Police never considered multiple offenders."

December 2008 language explicitly said **killer or killers**.
