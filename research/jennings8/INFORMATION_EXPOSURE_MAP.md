# Jennings 8 — Information Exposure / Retaliation Pathway Map

## Purpose

This file tests a narrower and more useful version of the "they knew too much" theory.

A retaliation theory requires more than showing that a victim possessed information.

For each woman, we need to establish four separate links:

1. **INFORMATION** — what did she actually know?
2. **DISCLOSURE** — did she tell police, family, a prosecutor, another victim, or someone else?
3. **EXPOSURE** — who could plausibly know that she had disclosed it?
4. **RETALIATION LINK** — is there evidence that her death was connected to that knowledge?

Only when all four links have evidence does a witness-elimination theory become substantially stronger.

Evidence tiers:
- **A — official / records-based**
- **B — contemporaneous named family/witness**
- **C — later investigative journalism based on claimed case files**
- **D — later documentary / secondhand claim**
- **E — theory only**

No entry identifies a killer.

# Who could know that victims were talking?

This is the critical second-level map.

Potential exposure channels:

## A. Investigators / law enforcement
If a woman was formally interviewed or used as CI:
- interviewer;
- supervisor;
- task-force case team;
- records/evidence personnel;
- prosecutor.

This does not imply wrongdoing. It defines who had legitimate knowledge of cooperation.

## B. Criminal defendants / defense counsel
If a witness became part of a filed prosecution:
- discovery obligations may reveal identity/statements;
- subpoenas or hearings may make cooperation visible.

Need case-specific records.

## C. Social network
In a small, overlapping network:
- victims may tell friends/family;
- suspects may learn who is "talking";
- informal CI activity may become known.

This is plausible but must be sourced victim by victim.

## D. Informant handlers
If the victim was a documented CI:
- handler identity is essential.
- CI records may show target, dates and payments/contacts.

## E. Jail environment
For Necole's 2002 witness role:
- officers charged/investigated;
- other inmates/witnesses;
- prosecutors/defense.

Need discovery and court files to determine actual exposure.

# What would prove a retaliation pathway?

The theory becomes materially stronger if we find:

1. victim gave specific information naming a person;
2. that person learned she had cooperated;
3. threat/intimidation followed;
4. she was killed soon after;
5. physical/phone/vehicle evidence ties the threatened person or associate to her final movements.

Without #2–#5, "she knew too much" remains motive speculation.

# Analytical conclusion

The public evidence does **not** currently prove a systematic witness-elimination conspiracy.

It does show something narrower and important:

> Several victims had fear or reported knowledge concerning earlier violence, and Necole is independently documented as having previously cooperated in a serious law-enforcement misconduct investigation.

The next question is not simply:
> "What did they know?"

It is:
> **"Who knew that they knew it?"**
