# Jennings 8 — Political & Institutional Ledger

## Purpose

Track political/public-office connections without turning political controversy into unsupported murder allegations.

Rule:
**A political connection is not evidence of murder, obstruction, or cover-up.**

Every political claim must record:
- who made it;
- what evidence supports it;
- denial/response;
- whether any law-enforcement finding exists.

---

## Charles W. Boustany Jr.

Context:
- then-U.S. Representative from Louisiana;
- later a candidate for U.S. Senate in 2016.

### Ethan Brown allegations
Brown's 2016 book cited anonymous sources alleging Boustany had been a client of sex workers including some of the Jeff Davis 8.

Brown also reported that **Martin Guillory**, a Boustany field staffer, had a business/ownership connection to the Boudreaux Inn.

### Critical limitation
Brown explicitly wrote/reported that there was **no evidence Boustany or Martin Guillory had involvement in the murders**.

### Boustany response
Boustany and his campaign denied the prostitution allegations.

He filed a defamation lawsuit against Ethan Brown and Simon & Schuster in 2016. The lawsuit was later dropped after the election.

### Why it belongs in the series
- demonstrates how the Boudreaux Inn connected the local case to a national political controversy;
- raises legitimate questions about social/political proximity to the locations and people in Brown's investigation;
- shows how true-crime reporting became entangled in an election.

### What Docket Unseen must NOT say
- Boustany participated in a murder;
- Boustany covered up the murders;
- the Boudreaux Inn connection proves political protection.

No reliable evidence reviewed establishes those propositions.

Sources:
- Washington Post, Sept. 2016
- Talking Points Memo, Sept. 2016
- Vice Ethan Brown interview
- AP coverage of defamation suit

Status:
**DISPUTED NON-MURDER ALLEGATION / POLITICAL CONTROVERSY.**

## Martin "Big G" Guillory

Brown reported:
- Guillory had an ownership/operational connection to the Boudreaux Inn;
- he later served as a field representative for Boustany.

Boustany's staff said he had not known about Guillory's motel involvement.

Guillory reportedly told Brown he did not know prostitution was occurring at the inn.

Research needed:
- property/company records;
- exact ownership/lease dates;
- employment dates with Boustany;
- police calls/arrest records at Boudreaux Inn;
- whether any official task-force investigation focused on the connection.

Status:
**DOCUMENTABLE BUSINESS/POLITICAL CONNECTION; MURDER LINK NOT ESTABLISHED.**

## Louisiana Attorney General Buddy Caldwell

In February 2014, public pressure questioned why the Louisiana Attorney General did not take over the Jeff Davis 8 investigation.

Caldwell told KPLC:
- the Attorney General's Office had "zero jurisdiction and control" to take over the investigation;
- his office had representatives assisting the task force;
- the Jefferson Davis Parish Sheriff's Office retained primary jurisdiction.

Why it matters:
- helps explain the institutional limits of state intervention;
- counters a simplistic claim that the AG could simply seize control of the case but chose not to.

Sources:
- KPLC, "Attorney General addresses Jeff Davis Eight"
- KPLC, "Jeff Davis 8: Why A.G. Buddy Caldwell doesn't intervene"

Status:
**DOCUMENTED OFFICIAL POSITION.**

## Local elected sheriff structure

The sheriff is an elected office in Louisiana.

Core administrations to document:
- Ricky Edwards — sheriff during core killings/task-force formation;
- Ivy Woods — later sheriff;
- Kyle Miers — assumed office in 2024 and current sheriff in 2026.

Important:
Criticism of an elected sheriff or department is not automatically a partisan/political claim. Focus on documented investigative conduct, public statements, and institutional responsibility.
