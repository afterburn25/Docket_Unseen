# Jennings 8 — Theory Ledger

This file is for **theories**, not conclusions.

## Theory 1 — One common offender / serial killer

### Support
- victims came from overlapping social circles;
- bodies were dumped in rural waterways/roads around Jennings, with Necole later found off I-10 in Acadia Parish;
- task force in 2008–09 publicly treated a common offender as a serious working theory;
- Aug. 2009 task-force profile used singular "offender" repeatedly.

### Problems / counterpoints
- not all known injuries/circumstances are identical;
- arrests/charges in individual cases pointed investigators toward more than one possible offender;
- Brown later argued the single-killer model did not fit the case-file evidence.

Status: **OFFICIAL EARLY WORKING THEORY / UNRESOLVED.**

## Theory 2 — Multiple offenders within the same social/criminal network

### Support
- Brown's reporting argues several different people may have been involved;
- Patterson's case generated specific murder charges against two men before dismissal;
- Lopez's case generated arrests/warrants involving different people;
- victims, witnesses, suspects, informants, and law enforcement existed in a highly overlapping small-town network.

### Problems
- requires case-by-case proof that is currently absent;
- some alleged links come from unstable or disputed witnesses.

Status: **JOURNALISTIC THEORY / UNRESOLVED.**

## Theory 3 — Victims were targeted because of information they possessed

### Documented fear pattern
Separate from the motive theory itself, several victims have sourced fear statements:
- Muggy Brown's sister told KPLC Muggy believed "her time had come";
- Brittney Gary's mother said Brittney had expressed concern for her safety and no longer knew whom she could trust;
- Necole Guillory's mother said Necole repeatedly told family she would not live long.

See `FEAR_AND_FOREKNOWLEDGE.md`.

These statements make the theory worth investigating, but do not prove motive.

Variants include:
- drug-trade knowledge;
- police-informant activity;
- knowledge of other murders;
- Leonard Crochet shooting/witness information;
- knowledge of alleged law-enforcement misconduct.

Support:
- Brown says all eight had informant relationships.
- Some victims were reportedly questioned about other deaths.
- Family/witness accounts describe fear before some deaths.

Problems:
- "all eight informants" must be verified victim-by-victim;
- Brown later expressed uncertainty about how many victims were actually present for the Crochet shooting;
- motive remains unproven.

Status: **UNRESOLVED THEORY.**

## Theory 4 — Law-enforcement involvement / cover-up

### Sources advancing it
- some victim relatives;
- witnesses quoted in task-force materials as described by Brown;
- Ethan Brown's reporting;
- later documentaries.

### Counterpoint
- law enforcement has denied involvement.
- investigative failure, conflicts, or misconduct do not by themselves establish participation in murder.

Status: **DISPUTED THEORY.**

## Theory 5 — Copycat / serial dumper / mixed causes

Possibility that:
- one or more original killings created a disposal pattern others copied;
- some killings were unrelated but victims' shared social networks and disposal environments made them appear connected;
- one offender may have killed some but not all.

Status: **UNRESOLVED THEORY.**

## Research rule

Every theory episode must show:
- evidence cited in favor;
- evidence against;
- origin of the theory;
- official response;
- what remains unverified.
