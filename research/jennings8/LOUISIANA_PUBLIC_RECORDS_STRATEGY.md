# Jennings 8 — Louisiana Public Records Strategy

## Purpose

The Jennings 8 murders remain active/open investigations. Broad requests for investigative files are therefore likely to trigger Louisiana's law-enforcement-record exemptions.

The strategy should be:

1. request **records that current Louisiana law expressly treats as public**;
2. separately request **reasonably segregable non-exempt portions** of broader files;
3. ask agencies to identify the statutory basis for anything withheld;
4. use exact case numbers, dates, names, and document types;
5. do not treat a denial of an active investigative file as evidence of concealment.

This is research strategy, not legal advice.

---

## Current Louisiana law — R.S. 44:3

Official current text:
https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/LawPrint.aspx?d=99685

R.S. 44:3(A)(1) generally allows law-enforcement/prosecutive agencies to withhold records pertaining to pending criminal litigation or criminal litigation reasonably anticipated until final adjudication or settlement.

R.S. 44:3 also protects confidential-source identities and certain investigative/security information.

### Records expressly public under R.S. 44:3(A)(4)

The statute says the following remain public records:

- the **initial report** of the investigating officer(s);
- **booking records** under Code of Criminal Procedure Article 228;
- records of issuance of a summons or citation;
- records of filing of a **bill of information**.

Important:
The statute distinguishes the initial investigating report from follow-up/subsequent investigative reports.

---

## What the initial report can contain

Current R.S. 44:3(A)(4)(b) requires the initial report to include, subject to other law:

- narrative description of the alleged offense;
- name/identification of each suspect charged with or arrested for the offense;
- time and date of alleged offense;
- location;
- property involved;
- vehicles involved;
- names of investigating officers.

A 2025 amendment removed the prior statutory requirement that the initial report list every witness.

Effective date of that amendment: August 1, 2025.

Legislative summary:
https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1430040

## Booking records

Louisiana Code of Criminal Procedure Art. 228 says booking records/book and booking information summaries are open for public inspection.

Official:
https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=112381

This is important for:
- Frankie Richard;
- Hannah Conner;
- Tracee Chaisson;
- Byron Chad Jones;
- Lawrence Nixon.

Booking records may resolve:
- booking date/time;
- exact charge;
- arresting agency;
- other persons arrested in same event;
- whether a murder-related charge actually appeared at booking.

They do **not** by themselves establish continuous custody.

## Louisiana Legislative Auditor guidance

The Louisiana Legislative Auditor's Public Records Law FAQ (revised August 2025) says investigative records held by police, district attorneys, attorney general, etc. can be exempt while criminal litigation is pending.

It also notes investigative video can remain confidential until final adjudication/settlement.

FAQ:
https://app.lla.la.gov/llala.nsf/vwFAQsPublicRecordsLawFAQ/Public_Records_Law_FAQ/%24FILE/Public%20Records%20Law%20FAQ.pdf

This means requests for:
- Brittney surveillance held as evidence;
- raw interview videos in an active case;
- body/dash footage tied to an open homicide;
- task-force witness interviews;

may be withheld even if the underlying event is old.

Use station archives / voluntarily released copies when possible.

## D. Confidential informant records

R.S. 44:3 protects confidential-source identities.

Therefore:
- direct CI files may be especially difficult to obtain;
- do not expect agencies to disclose confidential-source identity simply because the alleged informant is deceased.

Alternative routes:
- court filings;
- witness lists;
- disclosed discovery;
- family interviews;
- officer testimony/depositions;
- closed criminal/civil cases;
- previously released records.

## E. Coroner/autopsy records

These are not necessarily governed by the same active-police-file exemption in the same way, but availability can vary by custodian and other law.

Request victim by victim from the actual coroner/autopsy custodian.

Ask separately for:
- cause/manner;
- autopsy;
- toxicology;
- identification method;
- date/time of examination.

# Suggested wording — public core records

> Pursuant to Louisiana Public Records Law, including R.S. 44:3(A)(4), I request the public initial investigating-officer report, booking record under Code of Criminal Procedure Article 228, and any filed bill of information relating to [person/event/case].
>
> I am not requesting confidential-source information or exempt follow-up investigative material in this portion of the request.
>
> If any requested record is withheld, please identify the statutory basis for withholding and produce all reasonably segregable non-exempt portions.

# Records-law rules for the documentary

1. A records denial is **not evidence of guilt or cover-up**.
2. An "initial report" is not the full investigation.
3. Booking charge ≠ conviction.
4. Bill of information = prosecutor-filed charge, not proof beyond reasonable doubt.
5. Dismissal can reflect evidentiary insufficiency without proving innocence or guilt.
6. Confidential-source protection may prevent confirmation of informant theories.
7. Always preserve agency response letters in GitHub.

# Request mechanics under R.S. 44:32 / 44:35

Current Louisiana law requires a written determination when the custodian disputes whether a requested record is public.

R.S. 44:32(D):
- if a disclosure question is raised, the custodian must respond in writing within **five days**, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal public holidays;
- the response must state the determination and the legal basis for any exemption.

R.S. 44:35:
- after five days without a written determination or reasonable estimate for collection/segregation/redaction/review, enforcement remedies may become available.

Official:
- https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=99691
- https://www.legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=99703

Operational rule for Docket Unseen:
- log the exact date every request is received;
- preserve acknowledgement;
- preserve fee estimate;
- preserve any exemption citation;
- do not characterize delay/denial as misconduct without reviewing the statutory basis.

## Fees

R.S. 44:32:
- inspection/review of public records is generally not subject to a fee;
- copies may carry lawful fees;
- custodians may require copying fees in advance.

Ask for electronic copies where possible and request fee notice before production.
