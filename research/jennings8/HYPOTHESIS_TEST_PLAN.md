# Jennings 8 — Hypothesis Test Plan

## Purpose

This file turns the major theories into **testable questions**.

The goal is not to choose a theory by intuition. The goal is to identify which missing record would most strongly:
- support;
- weaken;
- or falsify

each explanation.

No theory receives a score or winner.

---

# Theory A — One common offender / serial killer

## Evidence that would strengthen it

1. Same unknown DNA profile in multiple victim cases.
2. Same vehicle linked independently to multiple victim windows.
3. Same phone/contact number appearing repeatedly before disappearances.
4. Same distinctive injury pattern in multiple autopsies.
5. Same offender-linked trace evidence across scenes.
6. One suspect independently placed with multiple victims shortly before death.
7. Common offender confession containing nonpublic details corroborated by records.
8. Geographic behavior consistent with one person's known movements.

## Evidence that would weaken it

1. Different known offenders proven in separate victim cases.
2. Distinct DNA profiles attributable to separate perpetrators.
3. Hard alibi/incarceration that rules a candidate out across subsets.
4. Clearly different causes/methods with victim-specific motives and no common link.
5. Independent prosecutions supported by strong physical evidence against different people.

## Highest-value missing records

- unknown DNA profiles;
- all autopsies;
- phone records;
- cross-case vehicle evidence;
- task-force suspect/exclusion logs.

---

# Theory B — Multiple offenders inside the same local network

## Evidence that would strengthen it

1. Patterson physical evidence strongly tied to Jones/Nixon or another distinct offender.
2. Kristen evidence independently tied to Richard/Conner/another separate cluster.
3. Different unknown DNA profiles in different victim cases.
4. Different vehicles/last-seen companions for different victims.
5. Victim-specific motives with no common offender overlap.
6. Witness accounts independently corroborated by physical evidence in separate cases.

## Evidence that would weaken it

1. One DNA profile across several cases.
2. One vehicle/contact repeatedly linked to multiple victims.
3. One offender possessing nonpublic details from multiple cases.
4. Strong common MO/signature across victims.

## New primary legal development — Nixon 2016 interview

A published 2018 Louisiana appellate opinion confirms Jefferson Davis Parish prosecutors sought homicide information from Lawrence Nixon in 2016.

This creates a new high-value test:
- identify the homicide;
- identify the "suspect" Nixon referenced;
- compare Nixon's 2016 account with his original 2005–06 statements;
- determine whether the box-cutter detail existed in original Patterson evidence or was new/general information.

If the homicide is confirmed as Patterson, the interview could materially clarify whether prosecutors viewed Nixon as:
- a possible participant;
- a witness;
- a source against another person;
- or some combination.

The appellate court says investigators found the 2016 information insufficiently credible/helpful.

## Highest-value records

- Patterson court/lab file;
- Kristen warrants/vehicle evidence;
- victim DNA comparisons;
- victim phone/contact histories.

# Theory C — Victims were targeted because they knew too much

## Evidence that would strengthen it

1. Formal records showing a victim was an informant/witness.
   - **Necole now partially satisfies this criterion:** AP review of state/FBI records confirms she was a witness in the 2002 parish-jail misconduct investigation. What remains unproven is whether that knowledge had any connection to her 2009 death.
2. Documented police interview about an earlier murder before her own death.
3. Explicit threat naming the information she possessed.
4. Multiple victims tied to the same confidential matter.
5. Witness statement saying victim was killed to prevent disclosure, corroborated independently.
6. Suspect communication demonstrating awareness of victim cooperation.

## Evidence that would weaken it

1. No CI/witness role for victims with fear statements.
2. Fear arose only after friends were dying, with no specific knowledge.
3. Alleged informant claims trace only to later rumor.
4. Victims' information concerned unrelated low-level matters with no plausible homicide motive.

## Highest-value records

- Necole's original 2002 interview / video and any 2009 task-force review of that history;
- CI files;
- police interview logs;
- prosecutor witness lists;
- threat reports;
- family contemporaneous statements;
- task-force witness reports.

# Theory D — Law-enforcement involvement / coordinated cover-up

## Evidence that would strengthen it

1. Direct evidence that an officer intentionally destroyed or concealed homicide evidence.
2. False official report contradicted by physical evidence.
3. Documented order to suppress a witness statement.
4. DNA/forensic match to an officer that was knowingly concealed.
5. Multiple independent witnesses describing same officer conduct, corroborated by records.
6. Financial/communication evidence of coordination.
7. Federal/state finding of obstruction tied specifically to the eight homicide cases.

## Evidence that would weaken it

1. Misconduct proves unrelated ethics/evidence issues but not homicide obstruction.
2. Outside agencies independently reviewed critical evidence and found no officer link.
3. DNA eliminated officers/personnel.
4. Alleged cover-up witnesses recanted or were contradicted by contemporaneous records.
5. Major investigative failures can be explained by decomposition, poor witnesses, or ordinary negligence.

## Highest-value records

- Ewing tapes/transcripts;
- FBI/AG disposition of cover-up allegations;
- officer DNA comparison results;
- evidence-room audits;
- Gary truck testing;
- internal-affairs / State Police reviews;
- CI files and handler assignments.

---

# Theory E — One or more law-enforcement conflicts impaired the case without murder involvement

This is distinct from Theory D.

## Evidence that would strengthen it

1. Proven ethics conflicts involving investigators.
2. Delayed testing / lost evidence tied to specific case decisions.
3. Personal relationships between investigators and victims/witnesses.
4. Evidence-room problems.
5. Witnesses refusing cooperation due mistrust.
6. Outside agencies identifying procedural weaknesses.

## Evidence that would weaken it

1. Primary case files show timely, documented, methodical handling.
2. Alleged failures are later misunderstandings.
3. Missing evidence claims are unrelated to these cases.

## Current public support

Already documented:
- Warren Gary ethics violation;
- Paula Guillory missing-evidence-money event;
- Ewing tapes routed outside local chain of command;
- public mistrust.

Status:
**This institutional-failure theory currently has more documented support than a proven murder-cover-up theory.**

That does not establish why the murders remain unsolved.

# Theory F — Mixed scenario

Possible model:
- one offender responsible for a subset;
- separate offenders for others;
- witness/informant motive in some;
- ordinary interpersonal/drug violence in others;
- investigative failures made unrelated cases look more unified.

## Evidence that would strengthen it

1. DNA clusters divide victims into subsets.
2. different suspect clusters become physically corroborated.
3. different causes/methods align with different people.
4. some victims share one transport/geographic pattern while others do not.
5. one named suspect is excluded from later cases but fits earlier ones.

## Evidence that would weaken it

1. one strong forensic signature crosses most/all victims.

---

# Theory G — Some deaths were not homicides

## Evidence that would strengthen it

1. autopsy/coroner files classify particular deaths as accidental/undetermined with no trauma.
2. toxicology provides a credible independent cause.
3. scene evidence contradicts body-dumping homicide theory.

## Evidence that would weaken it

1. homicide findings;
2. asphyxiation/sharp-force evidence;
3. evidence bodies were transported/dumped;
4. common offender evidence.

## Important language rule

The 2009 task force treated all eight as murders unless proven otherwise, while later Sheriff Ivy Woods said not all eight were formally ruled homicides.

Use victim-specific findings.

# Published task-force data-mining finding

The 2013 Helbich/Hagenauer/Leitner/Edwards paper analyzed 172 FBI Orion records related to Necole and found:
- three distinct textual clusters;
- Kristen/Brittney investigative material was closely related;
- Laconia/Muggy material was notably different;
- cluster geography differed between Jennings, Lafayette, and Lake Charles;
- the task force said the analysis surfaced previously unknown potentially important clues.

See `ORION_DATA_MINING_STUDY.md`.

This independently supports **testing victim subsets / sub-networks**, but it does not identify an offender and does not prove multiple killers.

# Falsification questions for each major lead

## Frankie Richard
What would materially weaken a broad Richard theory?
- verified alibi/custody for a victim window;
- DNA exclusion across relevant cases;
- last-seen records pointing elsewhere;
- no Richard contact in victim phone/vehicle records.

What would strengthen it?
- primary last-seen statements across multiple victims;
- vehicle/phone linkage;
- forensic evidence;
- corroborated nonpublic confession.

## Jones/Nixon
What would weaken?
- lab evidence excludes their alleged crime scene/vehicle;
- witness recantation shown unreliable;
- custody/alibi inconsistent with death window.

What would strengthen?
- physical evidence corroborating witness narrative;
- consistent independent statements;
- documented victim transport links.

## Danny Barry
What would weaken?
- original Brittney witness statement does not identify Barry;
- vehicle/alibi records exclude him;
- task-force interview/records show he was investigated and cleared on evidence.

What would strengthen?
- original independent witnesses;
- vehicle match;
- victim phone/contact evidence;
- forensic link.

## Law-enforcement cover-up
What would weaken?
- officer DNA exclusions;
- full outside-agency review of evidence with no suppression;
- inmate claims shown to be contradictory/hearsay.

What would strengthen?
- corroborated primary evidence of intentional evidence destruction/suppression tied to these homicides.

# Decision rule for Docket Unseen

We should only move from:
**"possible" → "probable"**

when at least two independent evidence classes converge, such as:
- witness + physical;
- phone + vehicle;
- DNA + timeline;
- court record + forensic report.

We should only move from:
**"probable" → "established"**

when:
- court/adjudication;
- official finding;
- or genuinely conclusive primary evidence

supports it.

No theory should be promoted because it is more dramatic.

## Necole-specific test — 2002 jail witness role

New documented fact:
- Necole was a witness in a 2002 Jefferson Davis Parish jail misconduct investigation that resulted in criminal pleas by former correction officers.

This strengthens only one proposition:
> Necole had documented firsthand knowledge of prior law-enforcement misconduct.

It does **not** establish:
> she was killed because of that knowledge.

### Evidence that would connect the 2002 case to motive
- threat referencing her 2002 testimony;
- later witness saying she was targeted over those statements;
- task-force motive memo connecting the cases;
- communication/contact with a 2002-case subject shortly before death;
- Necole naming the 2002 case when expressing fear.

### Evidence that would weaken the connection
- no later contact or threat;
- task force investigated and found no relevance;
- her fear concerned unrelated people/events;
- 2002 cooperation ended years earlier with no continuing dispute.

Primary files are required before moving beyond "documented knowledge."
