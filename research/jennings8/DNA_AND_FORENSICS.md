# Jennings 8 — DNA & Forensics Ledger

## Purpose

This file tracks what is actually known about biological evidence, DNA testing, autopsies, decomposition, and forensic limitations.

One of the most important questions in the entire case is:

> **What DNA evidence exists, from which victim/scene, and what happened when investigators compared it to law enforcement and persons of interest?**

Public reporting confirms DNA comparisons occurred, but not the results.

---

# 1. Law-enforcement DNA swabbing — 2009

KPLC Dec. 28, 2009 reported:
- officers working for agencies investigating the eight deaths were having DNA collected;
- Sheriff Ricky Edwards said DNA would be compared with **evidence from the cases**;
- Edwards said both former and current officers with involvement in the cases would be tested;
- private investigator Kirk Menard was also tested;
- Edwards said the testing process would be long and continuous.

Source:
https://www.kplctv.com/story/11737933/police-investigator-swabbed-for-dna-in-jeff-davis-case/

Status:
**CONTEMPORANEOUS OFFICIAL STATEMENT.**

## What this establishes

At minimum, investigators believed they had case evidence against which DNA elimination/comparison samples could be useful.

It does **not** establish:
- which victim's case contained the DNA;
- whether the DNA was semen, blood, skin cells, hair, or another source;
- whether there was one profile or multiple;
- whether it came from a perpetrator;
- whether any officer matched.

---

# 2. Why officers were swabbed

Edwards told KPLC the policy was meant in part to address public rumors that a law-enforcement officer might be involved.

Possible legitimate investigative purposes:
- eliminate investigators who had handled scenes/evidence;
- compare unknown profiles;
- rule out contamination;
- address specific suspicion;
- demonstrate transparency.

Without lab documentation, Docket Unseen should not speculate which purpose applied to each officer.

# 6. Decomposition limitations

Several bodies were recovered in advanced decomposition.

Official/later reporting says:
- Loretta — cause undetermined;
- Kristen — cause undetermined due decomposition;
- Whitnei — cause not determined in later summaries;
- Crystal required DNA identification after advanced decomposition.

Forensic consequences:
- cause/manner may be impossible to determine;
- time-of-death windows widen;
- biological material degrades;
- water exposure can destroy/transfer trace evidence;
- toxicology may be harder to interpret.

This is a major alternative explanation for weak cases that does not require misconduct.

# 8. Identification methods

Known:
- Crystal identified by DNA after significant decomposition.
- Brittney initially identified from scene/body characteristics and later confirmed by coroner.
- Others need exact official identification method documented.

Why this matters:
DNA used for identification is not necessarily perpetrator DNA.

The series must not imply:
> "There was DNA evidence in Crystal's case"

meaning offender DNA simply because DNA was used to identify her.
