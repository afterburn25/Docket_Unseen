# Russell Foote Cold Case Research

**Branch:** `research/russell-foote-cold-case`  
**Status:** Active research  
**Case:** Homicide of Russell "Russ" Foote  
**Date:** May 17, 1974  
**Location:** Lafayette, Louisiana  
**Investigating agency:** Lafayette Parish Sheriff's Office  
**Current public status:** Unsolved / no arrest publicly reported as of June 2025 reporting

## Purpose

This directory is a structured research workspace for the Russell Foote homicide. The goal is to reconstruct the case from public and obtainable primary records, identify gaps or contradictions, and document possible investigative leads without treating any uncharged person as guilty.

This is a research project, not a declaration that the case has been solved.

## Ground Rules

1. Separate established fact from reporting, recollection, allegation, theory, and inference.
2. Do not publicly identify an uncharged person as the killer.
3. Preserve source provenance for every material claim.
4. Prefer original records over later summaries.
5. Record contradictions rather than silently choosing one version.
6. Do not contact witnesses deceptively or interfere with the active investigation.
7. Any genuinely new evidentiary lead should be preserved and provided to the investigating agency before publication.
8. Personal addresses, phone numbers, and other sensitive information about private individuals should not be committed here unless already public and necessary for legitimate case research.

## Research Labels

- **CONFIRMED** — supported by a primary record or multiple strong independent sources.
- **REPORTED** — reported by a credible source but primary record not yet acquired.
- **WITNESS/FAMILY ACCOUNT** — attributed recollection that still needs corroboration.
- **ALLEGATION** — claim not established as fact.
- **THEORY** — analytical possibility only.
- **CONTRADICTION** — materially conflicting accounts.
- **MISSING RECORD** — a record reported to have existed but not yet located.
- **OPEN QUESTION** — unresolved research item.
