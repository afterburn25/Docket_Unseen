## Publicly Reported Crime-Scene Facts

According to KPLC's June 2025 report, sheriff's deputies found a white Chevrolet station wagon in the roadway facing east. The engine and air conditioner were running. Russell Foote was in the driver's seat and bleeding from the head.

KPLC reported that three shots were fired, two struck Foote, and no murder weapon or confession has been publicly identified. The report also states that financial ledgers were in the vehicle.

These details remain **REPORTED** until the original LPSO initial report and crime-scene records are obtained.

## Employment / Financial Context

KPLC reported that Foote became director/manager of the Lafayette Red Cross operation in 1968. His son, Kevin Foote, has said Russell had been reviewing accounting irregularities before the homicide and had restricted access to bank statements.

A former LPSO detective interviewed by KPLC, Guy Barnett, said he believed the motive was embezzlement and that the case was circumstantial. KPLC reported that a former Red Cross secretary had a later criminal history involving forgery, but also emphasized that she was never charged in Russell Foote's death and that a grand jury declined to indict her.

**Research rule:** Until primary records are acquired, the uncharged former employee is tracked here as `FORMER_RED_CROSS_SECRETARY`. Nothing in this repository should be read as a finding of guilt.

## Fire / Record-Loss Context

A family-authored investigative account by Kevin Foote reports:
- a small fire at the Red Cross office on April 9, 1974;
- a larger fire on April 26, 1974;
- a later inability to locate the historical arson-investigation file;
- destruction of physical evidence from the homicide case by a crime lab;
- difficulty locating a record of the grand-jury presentation.

Those assertions are important research leads but currently remain **WITNESS/FAMILY ACCOUNT** or **MISSING RECORD** claims until verified independently.

## Do Not Treat as Established

- that the homicide and fires were committed by the same person;
- that any Red Cross employee committed the homicide;
- that a law-enforcement or prosecutorial cover-up occurred;
- that missing files were intentionally removed;
- that later criminal conduct by any person proves involvement in the 1974 homicide.
