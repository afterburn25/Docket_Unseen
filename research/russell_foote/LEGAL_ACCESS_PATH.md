# Legal / Records Access Paths — Russell Foote

**Research note only; not legal advice.**

This file records the access routes that appear available under current Louisiana law and current agency procedures. Requirements can change and should be verified at the time of any request.

## 1. General-public route — LPSO initial report

Louisiana R.S. 44:3(A)(4) states that the initial report of the officer or officers investigating a complaint is a public record.

The statute specifies that the initial report should include:
- narrative description of the alleged offense;
- charged/arrested suspect identification where lawfully releasable;
- time/date;
- location;
- property involved;
- vehicles involved;
- investigating officers.

For this case, the initial report is the first target because it should establish the basic scene facts without requiring family status.

Current LPSO request channel:
- recordsrequest@lafayettesheriff.com
- Records Department: 337-236-5845
- current posted fee for an Initial Report: $10

Source: Louisiana R.S. 44:3; LPSO Records Division.

## 2. Immediate-family route — R.S. 44:3(F)

R.S. 44:3(F) provides a much broader route after ten years have elapsed from a death by other than natural causes.

After the required approval by the district court having jurisdiction over any criminal prosecution that may result from the death, agencies holding investigative files, evidence, potential evidence, records, documents, or other items relating to the death must provide copies/access to qualifying immediate family.

The statute defines immediate family for this subsection as:
- surviving spouse;
- children;
- grandchildren;
- siblings.

The access described by the statute includes examination, inspection, photographing, copying, testing, impressions, court use, and forensic studies.

### Designated researcher provision

Importantly for Docket Unseen, the statute says qualifying family may designate another person or persons for access to evidence/potential evidence/items after the statutory requirements are satisfied.

That means a lawful collaboration with Russell Foote's immediate family could provide a route to inspect source material that an ordinary public-record requester may not receive.

### Historical judicial interpretation

In *In re: Israel Trestman* (La. App. 4 Cir. 2001), the court discussed the ten-year immediate-family provision and upheld the family's access under the statute.

## 3. Victim/family review route — R.S. 44:3(K)

Current R.S. 44:3(K) also provides review/copy rights for certain victims or designated family members unless the agency certifies that the matter is subject to actual or reasonably anticipated criminal litigation, with additional provisions once prosecution has begun.

This is a separate provision from the ten-year mechanism and should be evaluated with counsel/agency guidance if the family participates.

## 4. Fire-record route

Lafayette Fire Department currently states that it produces a fire report for emergency responses and provides initial fire reports through its records office.

Current department contact:
- Lafayette Fire Department, Attn: Records
- 300 East Vermilion Street, Lafayette, LA 70501
- 337-291-8700
- LafayetteFire@LafayetteLA.gov
- current posted initial fire-report charge: $5

The department page says an investigative report and/or arrest report requires a subpoena under its current procedure.

However, R.S. 44:3(E) separately states that the Public Records Law is not to be construed as forbidding release of all or part of investigative files of fires classified as arson, incendiary, or suspicious unless, after consultation with the appropriate law-enforcement agency, a sheriff, district attorney, or other law-enforcement agency directs nondisclosure because of pending or anticipated criminal adjudication.

**Research approach:** request the initial fire reports first. If an investigative/arson file is withheld, ask for the written legal basis and preservation/retention information rather than assuming either entitlement or concealment.

If the family is willing to cooperate, the strongest research path may be:
1. ask what records they already possess;

5. coordinate any forensic-testing proposal with LPSO and the lawful evidence custodian.

## Boundaries

- Do not impersonate family or law enforcement.
- Do not seek secret grand-jury testimony through improper means.
- Do not remove, alter, or independently handle physical evidence without custodian authorization.
- Do not publicly disclose sensitive private information merely because it appears in a file.
- Do not characterize a records denial or destroyed record as a cover-up without evidence supporting intent.
